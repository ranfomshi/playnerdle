How the Wordle Game Works: The Rules in a Nutshell

The rules are gloriously simple. You have six attempts to guess a secret five-letter word. After each guess, every letter is colour-coded: green means the letter is correct and in the right position, yellow means it is in the word but in a different spot, and grey means it does not appear at all.

That feedback loop is the whole game. Each guess narrows the possibilities, and by your third or fourth attempt you are usually weighing two or three candidates. Solve it and you keep your streak alive; fail and you wait for tomorrow's word, plotting a smarter opening. There is no clock, no score multiplier, and nothing to buy — just you, the alphabet, and a little deduction.

One Puzzle a Day: The Secret Behind the Habit

What separates a daily word puzzle from the endless games filling app stores is restraint. You get exactly one puzzle per day — the same word for every player on the planet. That scarcity turns a quick brain-teaser into a ritual, like the morning cuppa or the crossword in the paper.

It also makes the game social. Because everyone faces the same challenge, comparing results is fair and instant. The famous emoji grid lets you share your solve without spoiling the answer, which is why family group chats across the UK still light up with rows of coloured squares before breakfast.

Play the Daily Word Puzzle Like a Pro

A few habits separate steady solvers from streak-breakers. Start with an opening word rich in common letters — something like RAISE, SLATE, or CRANE covers frequent vowels and consonants in one go. Use your second guess to test new letters rather than chasing an early green; information is worth more than a lucky stab in round two.

Remember that letters can repeat, which catches out even experienced players, and watch for common endings such as -ER, -LY, and -ING patterns in longer variants. Finally, slow down on the fifth guess. Most failed puzzles are lost to haste, not vocabulary — list every word that fits the clues before you commit, and your success rate will climb noticeably.
